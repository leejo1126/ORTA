An interphase nucleus contains **20–50 nuclear speckles**, visible as punctate structures
of variable size and irregular shape when stained for SR proteins (SRSF1/SRSF2). By EM they
are **interchromatin granule clusters**, one to several µm across, built of 20–25 nm
granules. The SC35 monoclonal antibody recognizes **SRSF2**. Structured-illumination
microscopy resolves substructure within speckles.

**Implication for detection.** Speckles are **larger, irregular, and fewer** than puncta —
favor detectors that tolerate non-round, variable-size bright bodies; use size/shape gating
and only modest watershed splitting (avoid fragmenting one speckle into many).
